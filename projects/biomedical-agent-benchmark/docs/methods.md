# Statistical methods and limits

The candidate uses person-specific MPQ extreme-state labels and a separate univariate LDA per device side. Each spectrum is normalized by its own 40–80 Hz mean. Candidate features are overlapping five-Hz bands with integer centers from 0 through 50 Hz. Clinical cutpoints come from the union of distinct available calibration surveys for each person. Band selection and fitting use calibration only; the selected score remains frozen during monitoring.

The reference averages normalized spectra within a device/survey match, checks agreement in duplicated labels and reconciles sides through the clinical survey identifier. It computes within-side AUC, averages sides within participant, and weights five participants equally. Numerical replay uses the charter's stated timing convention, not an inferred exact physiological transition instant.

The random comparison repeats fitting and band selection inside shuffled training folds. It retains calibration-defined pain labels and measures discrimination over the whole observed history. A separate control restricts the resulting out-of-fold scores to monitoring records: mean AUC is approximately 0.5053. Thus changes in the evaluated phase/population account for much of the full-history versus forward contrast. It would be inaccurate to attribute the entire gap to a single leakage mechanism.

Uncertainty diagnostics resample occupied time blocks jointly across each person's sides while conditioning on the fitted calibration workflow. At 1-, 7- and 30-day blocks, respectively, 1,842, 1,615 and 1,326 of 2,000 replicates retain both classes on every side. Finite percentile intervals condition on those retained replicates; they are not unconditional confidence intervals. Two participants contribute only three distinct high-pain monitoring surveys each. A source-person bootstrap alone misses within-series uncertainty.

Checked forward mean AUCs range from approximately 0.465 to 0.500 under the disclosed timing and preprocessing variants, preserving the technical rejection at the authored 0.60 point-estimate gate. This is not a test that the best possible decoder cannot work, a statement of analgesic efficacy, authorization to control stimulation, or a forecast for unseen patients. Long terminal observation gaps remain unidentified; no values are imputed into them.

Earlier project candidates applied or examined binary registry outcomes, Brier scoring and calibration, target-mixture standardization, competing-risk recorded-event yields, dependence-aware uncertainty, trial-power planning and hierarchical predictive assurance. Their source limitations, including incomplete historical registry snapshots and prior-sensitive decisions, motivated the more explicit scope of this published case study.
