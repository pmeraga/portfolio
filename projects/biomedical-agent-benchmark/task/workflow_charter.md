# Candidate workflow charter

The technical qualification policy requires a participant-balanced mean AUC of at least 0.60 for post-calibration monitoring. The terminal clinical/spectral records are original CC0 research data. This is not the published trial's efficacy analysis, its implanted controller, or a claim that no other decoder could work.

Candidate definition: a separate one-dimensional linear-discriminant score is fitted for each released device side. Its clinical labels are personalized low versus high MPQtotal, using the participant's 30th and 70th calibration percentiles. Use the NumPy linear quantile definition. Low includes scores <=the lower cutpoint; high is strictly >the upper cutpoint; the middle group is outside this extreme-state benchmark. Both hemispheres concern the same person's clinical state. Labels and scoring parameters are frozen at the clinical handover.

Candidate features: each spectrum is divided by its own mean power over 40-80 Hz, inclusive. Candidate features are mean normalized power in 5-Hz bands centered at the integer frequencies 0 through 50 Hz. Include existing frequency bins within center +/-2.5 Hz, clipping at the recorded frequency axis. Each clinical survey supplies one observation per side; multiple matched records of the same survey are averaged after spectrum normalization. Missing MPQ scores, nonfinite spectra, nonpositive normalization or unmatched survey IDs cannot supply a labeled observation. No outcome-based retrospective outlier deletion is part of the candidate.

Candidate fitting: the clinical pain cutpoints use the union of distinct available calibration surveys for each participant, with bilateral duplicates counted once. For each side, fit ordinary univariate LDA for each candidate feature using empirical class priors, class means and a pooled within-class variance (sum of squared within-class residuals divided by n-2). Select the band with highest calibration AUC of its fitted LDA decision score; choose the smallest center if tied. No monitoring observation may change the deployed score or clinical cutpoints. This describes the candidate object, not a mandated statistical validation method.

Qualification metric: calculate within-side AUC, average sides within participant, then average the five participant values equally. All five participants belong to the benchmark. This is a finite-panel technical screen, not a population efficacy or new-patient readiness criterion. The binding gate is the point estimate >=0.60; uncertainty still informs whether evidence is adequate and what further validation is needed.

Clock convention: source phase strings are local-naive, while HDF5 event times have an extraction offset. The numerical replay uses phase onset and endpoint plus 10 hours against HDF5 epoch seconds as the primary alignment. This is an explicit replay convention, not proof of an exact physiological transition time. Check +11 hours and at least +/-24 hours around the primary alignment. The source timestamps are deidentified; do not infer real calendar dates or identities. The qualification conclusion must not rely on a handful of boundary events.
